Architecture search and results

Jump to the complete search results ↓

The architecture search compared 37 configurations of a convolutional autoencoder across three stages. Each diagram below animates one of them, following a batch of six environmental variables over 6,935 days as the encoder compresses the record to five numbers and the decoder rebuilds it.

This page accompanies the Model architecture selection section of the results chapter. It provides animated architecture diagrams, complete architecture definitions and supplementary results for all three search stages.

How to read the diagrams

The encoder runs left to right along the top, the latent vector sits at the right-hand turn, and the decoder runs right to left along the bottom, so the reconstruction finishes directly underneath the input it is trying to reproduce.

strided convolution, halves the time axis dilated convolution, keeps the length, widens the receptive field pooling or interpolation fully connected bridge latent vector

The bar sliding above an active layer is the kernel. Its seven tick marks are the seven weights. When the dilation rate rises the marks fan apart while their number stays the same, which is the entire mechanism behind the wider receptive field. The traces in the input and output panels are illustrative, showing the shape of the task and not the plotted observations, while the measured reconstruction quality is the fraction of variance unexplained reported in the thesis.

The selected model

Three strided layers compress the record, then four dilated layers widen the view across more than two annual cycles before the bridge. This is the model carried into the clustering and regionalisation analyses.

Reconstruction of a real site

The figure below shows the reconstruction of the final model overlaid on the actual input.

Sites are ordered by how well the model reconstructed them, so the best and the worst cases are both available rather than only a flattering example. None of these sites was used for training, and the fraction of variance unexplained shown against each variable is the test-set figure for that channel.

Stage 1, encoder topology

Stage 1 asked whether the six variables should share one convolutional stack or receive one each. Sharing won despite reconstructing less accurately, because the separate stacks multiplied the bridge input sixfold and produced a lower effective-rank ratio and a higher condition number.

Stage 2, convolutional operator

Stage 2 held the topology fixed and varied how the temporal axis is processed and reduced. The eight configurations differ mainly in where the compression happens, which is visible in the diagrams as the point at which the slabs start to shorten.

The neural-network operators page explains the individual operations.

Parallel-branch fusion

Two of the Stage 2 architectures run a short-range strided branch alongside a long-range dilated branch and then have to join them. Concatenation stacks the two feature tensors and a 1 x 1 convolution mixes and reduces the channels at each position without widening the receptive field. Latent summation instead gives each branch its own bridge and adds the two embeddings. The choice is the reason the two parallel architectures differ in bridge size and parameter count.

Stage 3, refinement sweeps

Stage 3 varied one design choice at a time over the retained skeleton. Because the variants share a shape, they share a diagram: choose a sweep, then a configuration within it.
