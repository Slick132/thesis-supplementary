Supplement to the architecture-tuning appendix

Every architecture in the search, animated

The architecture search compared 37 configurations of a convolutional autoencoder across three stages. Each diagram below animates one of them, following a batch of six environmental variables over 6,935 days as the encoder compresses the record to five numbers and the decoder rebuilds it. Tensor shapes and receptive fields are computed from the convolution arithmetic rather than drawn by hand, so the picture agrees with the thesis tables.

How to read the diagrams

Every diagram is a hairpin. The encoder runs left to right along the top, the latent vector sits at the right-hand turn, and the decoder runs right to left along the bottom, so the reconstruction finishes directly underneath the input it is trying to reproduce.

strided convolution, halves the time axis dilated convolution, keeps the length, widens the view pooling or interpolation, no learned weights fully connected bridge latent vector

The bar sliding above an active layer is the kernel. Its seven tick marks are the seven weights. When the dilation rate rises the marks fan apart while their number stays the same, which is the entire mechanism behind the wider receptive field. Each diagram plays when it scrolls into view and can be paused or scrubbed. The traces in the input and output panels are illustrative, showing the shape of the task rather than plotted observations, while the measured reconstruction quality is the fraction of variance unexplained reported in the thesis.

Building blocks of the architectures

The full diagrams run at 6,935 timesteps, where individual values and kernel positions cannot be resolved. This section introduces each operator at a toy scale first, in the order the data meets them, so the architecture diagrams that follow read as assemblies of parts already understood.

1. Convolution over a multichannel series

A filter slides along time and reads every input variable at each position, so one output value combines information across variables and across neighbouring days. A layer holds several filters, and each produces its own feature map. The feature maps are learned descriptors rather than reconstructed environmental variables, which matters because a later diagram showing 256 channels is not showing 256 weather variables.

Each filter holds a separate kernel for every input variable, so the weights of this layer form a block of shape 3 x 2 x 3, being the number of output feature maps, the number of input variables and the kernel width. The animation uses a width-three kernel with stride one and a single zero-padding cell at each end, which is why the 12 positions survive the layer. Without that padding a width-three kernel would leave 12 - 3 + 1 = 10 positions.

aq,t = bq + ∑c=1C ∑j=1k wq,c,j x˜ c,t+j− k+12

Two variables over twelve days and three filters. The tilde marks the zero-padded input, and the highlighted window is the current kernel position and the value beneath is the output after the activation.

2. Nonlinear activation

Every convolution and every hidden fully connected layer is followed by a GELU activation. Without the activation the whole encoder would collapse to a single linear map, and stacking layers would add no expressive power. The values in the animation above are already passed through GELU, which is why some pre-activation sums arrive at the feature map reduced or near zero.

hq,t = GELU ( aq,t )

3. Strided convolution and same-padding

A strided layer steps the kernel two positions at a time, so the output holds one position for every two inputs. Padding cells at both ends let the kernel centre sit on the first and last day. The overlapping windows consult every input position, but the smaller output is still a compressed representation and may discard information that the learned features do not retain.

Lout = ⌊ Lin+2p−d (k−1) −1 s ⌋ +1 with k=7, s=2, d=1, p=3: Lout= ⌈ Lin2 ⌉

4. Dilated convolution

A dilated layer keeps the stride at one and spaces the seven weights apart by the dilation rate. The number of weights never changes, the span the kernel covers grows with the rate, and the output length is preserved because the padding is adjusted to match. Stacking rates of one, two, four and eight is what carries the selected model to a receptive field of 763 days without adding parameters.

weights=k=7 kernel span=d (k−1) +1

5. Growth of the receptive field

Each layer widens the span of the input record that a single output position depends on. Strided layers double the reach because the positions they read are already spaced apart, and dilated layers add their own spacing on top. The selected model stacks three strided layers and four dilated layers, which is what carries one feature position from seven days to 763 days, a little over two annual cycles.

The receptive field is the reach of one convolutional position before the bridge. After the pooling and the fully connected layers, every latent dimension depends on the whole record.

6. Adaptive average pooling

Dilated stacks reach the bridge still holding their full length, so a separate reduction is needed. Adaptive average pooling divides the time axis into a fixed number of bins and averages within each, reducing temporal positions while leaving the number of feature channels untouched. Global average pooling is the same idea with a single bin, so the position of a feature within the sequence is no longer represented explicitly, which is why the Dilated A configuration failed to reconstruct.

7. Flattening and the fully connected bridge

The convolutional stack produces features at many temporal positions, but the regionalisation needs one vector per location. The bridge is where that happens. The feature tensor is flattened into a single long vector, a hidden layer with GELU mixes it, and a final linear layer produces the five-dimensional embedding. Every latent dimension therefore depends on the whole record, not on one part of it.

h=GELU ( W1v+b1 ) z=W2h +b2

The shape chain along the top is the real one used by the selected model. The small network below carries toy values through four inputs, three hidden units and two latent units so the arithmetic is visible.

8. Decoder bridge and reshape

Reconstruction begins by reversing the change in shape, expanding the embedding back to a feature tensor the convolutional decoder can work on. The mapping is separately learned rather than an inverse of the encoder bridge, so the decoder is free to use different weights to rebuild what the encoder compressed.

Flattening and reshaping only rearrange values. The order is fixed, so the two operations are exact inverses of one another.

9. Linear interpolation

Where the encoder pooled the time axis, the decoder has to restore the length before the convolutional layers can run. Linear interpolation fills the gaps between neighbouring feature values. It carries no weights, changes only the temporal resolution, and does not recover values that pooling discarded.

10. Transposed convolution

The transposed convolution is the decoder counterpart of the strided layer. Each input position multiplies the entire kernel and scatters its contributions across several output positions, and where those contributions overlap they are added. The operation increases the temporal length when the stride exceeds one, learns its own weights, and is the transpose of the corresponding linear operation rather than an inverse convolution, so it cannot guarantee recovery of information lost during downsampling.

Lout= (Lin−1)s −2p +d(k−1) +pout +1

11. The 1 x 1 convolution

A 1 x 1 convolution looks at all feature channels at one temporal position and learns a new weighted combination of those channels. The operator changes the number of feature maps without combining neighbouring days, so the temporal length and receptive field stay unchanged. The parallel-concatenation architecture used the operator to reduce 256 feature channels to 128 before the fully connected bridge.

yq,t =bq+ ∑c=1Cin wq,c xc,t

The animation uses four input and three output channels so the calculation remains visible. The evaluated architecture applied the same operation to 256 input and 128 output channels.

12. Parallel-branch fusion

Two of the Stage 2 architectures run a short-range strided branch alongside a long-range dilated branch and then have to join them. Concatenation stacks the two feature tensors and a 1 x 1 convolution mixes and reduces the channels at each position without widening the receptive field. Latent summation instead gives each branch its own bridge and adds the two embeddings. The choice is the reason the two parallel architectures differ in bridge size and parameter count.

13. Variational sampling

The variational models replace the single projection to the embedding with two, one producing a mean and one a log variance, and then draw the embedding from that distribution. The draw is written so that the randomness enters through a separate standard normal variable, which keeps the network trainable by gradient descent. This block is the only structural difference between the deterministic and variational diagrams, and it accounts for the small difference in their parameter counts.

z=μ +σ⊙ε ε∼ N(0,I)

The grey dot is the mean and the shaded band spans one standard deviation. The moving marker is a fresh draw. In the trained models the standard deviations stayed close to zero, so the variational encoder behaved almost deterministically.

The selected model

Three strided layers compress the record, then four dilated layers widen the view across more than two annual cycles before the bridge. This is the model carried into the clustering and regionalisation analyses.

Reconstruction of a real site

Everything above shows how the operators work. This shows what the selected model actually produced. The series below are the observed record and the model reconstruction for one held-out test site, over the full 6,935 days, converted back to physical units. Choose a site, choose a window, and scroll through the record.

Sites are ordered by how well the model reconstructed them, so the best and the worst cases are both available rather than only a flattering example. None of these sites was used for training, and the fraction of variance unexplained shown against each variable is the test-set figure for that channel.

What to look for. Temperature and humidity track closely enough that the two lines are hard to separate at a one-year window. Wind is noticeably rougher. Precipitation is where the model visibly departs from the record, because rain falls on roughly 16 per cent of days, so the series is mostly zero with occasional large values, and the reconstruction smooths the peaks downward while spreading small amounts onto dry days.

Stage 1, encoder topology

Stage 1 asked whether the six variables should share one convolutional stack or receive one each. Sharing won despite reconstructing less accurately, because the separate stacks multiplied the bridge input sixfold and produced a latent space that failed the effective-rank check.

Stage 2, convolutional operator

Stage 2 held the topology fixed and varied how the temporal axis is processed and reduced. The eight configurations differ mainly in where the compression happens, which is visible in the diagrams as the point at which the slabs start to shorten.

Stage 3, refinement sweeps

Stage 3 varied one design choice at a time over the retained skeleton. Because the variants share a shape, they share a diagram: choose a sweep, then a configuration within it.
