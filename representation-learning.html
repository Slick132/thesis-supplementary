Representation learning

Representation learning transforms observations into features used by a later analysis. This page provides the extended background supporting the regionalisation framework.

Feature transformation

The performance of a machine-learning method depends partly on the representation supplied to it. Let X denote an input space containing observations such as feature vectors, time series or images. A feature transformation maps an observation x to a representation z in a feature space Z.

z=φ(x),x∈𝒳,z∈𝒵

The transformation can be designed manually through feature engineering or learned from the observations. A transformation learned from data is referred to as representation learning or feature learning.

Representation learning is often associated with dimensionality reduction, but fewer dimensions are not required. A learned representation can contain fewer, the same number of or more dimensions than the input. The undercomplete autoencoder in the thesis deliberately produces a lower-dimensional representation.

Role in environmental regionalisation

The representation connects the environmental observations to the clustering algorithm. The complete relationship is expressed as

{x}i=1n→φ{z}i=1n→𝒜{c}i=1n

Here, 𝒜 is the clustering procedure and ci is the environmental region assigned to site i. The transformation affects the regionalisation because the clustering algorithm has access only to the information and geometry present in the learned feature space.

Invariant features

Invariant features reduce sensitivity to variation considered irrelevant for a particular task. A face-recognition representation, for example, can reduce sensitivity to changes in lighting. Invariance is useful when irrelevant variation is known beforehand, but information removed for one task could be required for another.

Environmental regionalisation does not have one response variable that determines which variation is irrelevant. Temperature, precipitation, humidity, wind and their temporal patterns can all distinguish locations. The autoencoder was therefore not trained to remove variation from any environmental channel.

Disentanglement

Disentanglement aims to separate the factors responsible for variation in the observations while retaining the information required by the model. A representation of a video could, for example, separate identity, movement and camera position.

A standard reconstruction objective does not automatically produce disentangled dimensions. Even when specialised objectives are used, one learned dimension is not guaranteed to correspond uniquely to one physical factor. The autoencoder in the thesis had no disentanglement objective. Information about one environmental factor could consequently be distributed across several embedding dimensions.

Evaluation of a learned representation

The objective of the thesis was to obtain a compact representation that retained information from the multivariate climate series and arranged locations in a form suitable for comparison and clustering. Reconstruction error alone could not establish this property. A model could reconstruct dominant or smoothly varying channels while retaining less information about other environmental signals.

The final embedding was therefore evaluated through environmental-information recovery and geometry diagnostics. The recovery analysis tested whether environmental features remained predictable from the embedding. The geometry analysis tested whether distances between nearby embeddings remained meaningful after decoding.
