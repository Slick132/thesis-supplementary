Deep embedded clustering

Deep embedded clustering first trains a denoising autoencoder to reconstruct the input. The decoder is then discarded, and K-means initialises cluster centres in the learned embedding. The encoder and cluster centres are subsequently adjusted using a clustering loss.

1Pretrain a denoising autoencoder.
2Initialise centres with K-means.
3Optimise assignments and centres jointly.

Soft assignments and target distribution

Let zi denote embedding i and μj denote cluster centre j. A Student t kernel converts distance to a soft assignment:

qij=(1+‖zi−μj‖2/α)−(α+1)/2∑j′K(1+‖zi−μj′‖2/α)−(α+1)/2

The target distribution increases the contribution of high-confidence assignments and compensates for cluster frequency. Optimisation minimises the Kullback-Leibler divergence between the target distribution P and assignment distribution Q.

LDEC=DKL(P‖Q)=∑i,jpijlogpijqij

Training procedure

  1. Train the denoising autoencoder and retain the encoder.
  2. Encode the complete dataset and initialise K centres with K-means.
  3. Calculate the soft assignments and target distribution.
  4. Update the encoder and centres by minimising the clustering loss.
  5. Recalculate assignments at fixed intervals and stop when few labels change.

Improved deep embedded clustering

Improved deep embedded clustering retains the decoder during clustering. The objective combines the clustering loss with reconstruction error:

LIDEC=Lrecon+γDKL(P‖Q)

The reconstruction term discourages the embedding from changing solely to sharpen cluster assignments. The coefficient γ controls the balance between reconstruction and clustering.

Principal difference

Deep embedded clustering discards the decoder before cluster refinement. Improved deep embedded clustering keeps the decoder and continues to penalise reconstruction error. The extension therefore attempts to retain more of the input structure while the clusters are sharpened.
